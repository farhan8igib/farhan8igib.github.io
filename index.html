About

Building bridges between biology and behaviour.

I am an Associate Professor in the College of Health & Life Sciences at Hamad Bin Khalifa University, where I founded Qatar's first experimental behavioural neuroscience research centre.

My research brings together biotechnology, neuroscience and computation to investigate neurological and psychiatric disorders. I want to understand how genetic and molecular changes reach up into neural circuits and shape what an animal does.

The lab pairs human cohorts with functional disease models and quantitative behaviour. The long-term aim is simple to state and hard to reach: find the mechanisms and biomarkers that make treatment more precise.

Research

Four questions, one shared method.

The lab moves between human data, functional genetics, behavioural neuroscience and computation. Different tools, the same aim: connect a molecular change to something measurable in a living system.

Behavioural neuroscience

Reading behaviour, quantitatively

Precise, high-throughput approaches to learning, memory, sleep, sensorimotor gating and social behaviour, mostly in Drosophila.

Human genomics & multi-omics

Signatures of disease

Genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic and epigenetic signals of neurological and psychiatric disorders in Gulf and South Asian cohorts.

Functional neurogenetics

From candidate gene to circuit

Using fly models and optogenetics to test what a gene actually does to neural circuits and behaviour, not just what it correlates with.

AI & precision medicine

Making the data usable

Machine learning to prioritise biomarkers, characterise disease biology and speed up the search for therapeutics.
