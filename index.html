I am a behavioural neuroscientist at Hamad Bin Khalifa University, where I founded Qatar's first experimental behavioural neuroscience research centre. My work asks how genes shape brain function and behaviour, and it runs from human patient cohorts through functional genetics in Drosophila to machine learning.

Schizophrenia, autism, cerebral palsy, depression and Parkinson's disease sit at the centre of the lab. Before Doha I trained at IGIB in Delhi, then Oxford, A*STAR in Singapore, and Duke-NUS. The habit that stuck: let the biological question choose the method.
